The plyometric ladder is the plan

No prior jumping experience plus a right calf with history means the dosing matters far more than the exercise choice. Start at Phase 1 regardless of how easy it feels, and only move up when the gate conditions are met.

The plyometric ladder

Four phases, four weeks each at minimum. The number that matters is ground contacts — every landing is a dose of force through the calf and shin, and beginners get hurt by taking too many too soon, not by jumping too high. Contact counts exclude the low-level drills.

1Absorb

Learning to land

Weeks 1–4 · ~48 contacts per session

  • Pogo hops, double leg, in place — 3 × 12. Knees nearly straight, all ankle, minimum time on the ground.
  • Countermovement jump, stick the landing — 3 × 4. Moderate height, land soft, freeze two full seconds before the next rep.
  • Ankling drill — 2 × 20m. Low intensity, doesn't count toward contacts.

Gate to Phase 2: two consecutive weeks with no calf or shin awareness the following morning.

2Rebound

Adding elasticity

Weeks 5–8 · ~66 contacts per session

  • Pogo hops, continuous — 3 × 16. Now chained rather than reset between reps.
  • Box jump, step down — 3 × 5. Box 30–40cm. Always step off, never jump down.
  • Lateral hop and stick — 2 × 4 each side. First single-leg landings, with no rebound.

Gate to Phase 3: same two clean weeks, and landings still quiet by the last set.

3One leg

Running is a single-leg sport

Weeks 9–12 · ~84 contacts per session

  • Single-leg pogo hops in place — 3 × 8 each side.
  • Broad jump and stick — 3 × 4. Distance, not height. Land and hold.
  • Lateral bound and stick — 3 × 4 each side. Push sideways, land on the outside leg, hold.

Gate to Phase 4: two clean weeks and no side-to-side difference you can feel in the single-leg work.

4Elastic

Reactive strength

Weeks 13 onward · ~100 contacts per session

  • Single-leg hops travelling forward — 3 × 10 each side.
  • Alternating bounds — 3 × 6 each side. Exaggerated running stride, float between contacts.
  • Low hurdle hops — 3 × 6. Hurdles around 20cm, continuous.

This is the ceiling. Depth jumps and drop jumps sit above this and you don't need them. Stay here and get better at it.

Rules that keep the jumping safe

These matter more than the exercise selection. Plyometrics is the one part of this plan with a real injury cost if the dosing goes wrong.

Every session

How to run the drills

  • Plyos go immediately after the warm-up, before you touch a barbell. Never after squats.
  • Land quiet. Noise is force you failed to absorb.
  • If ground contact slows or landings get loud, that set is over. Rep quality is the stimulus, not rep count.
  • Rest 45–60 seconds between sets. This is not conditioning.
  • Trainers on, not racing flats.
When to back off

The right-side rule

  • Any calf or shin awareness the next morning: repeat the phase, don't advance.
  • Awareness two weeks running: drop back a phase for two weeks.
  • Pain during a landing: stop the session's plyos entirely, keep the lifting.
  • Never on consecutive days, and never within 24 hours before a quality run.
  • After any layoff of two weeks or more: restart one phase lower.

When the clock beats you

Both days now run close to an hour. The failure mode is rushing the compounds to make room for the accessories, so the cut order is decided in advance rather than in the moment.

Day B · cut in this order

Arms go first

  • 1. Hammer curls and tricep extensions.
  • 2. Face pulls.
  • 3. Planks — move them to legs day.
  • Bench, pull-ups, rows and the overhead press are what the day is for. They don't get cut.
Day A · cut in this order

Protect the lower leg work

  • 1. Planks.
  • 2. Standing single-leg calf raise.
  • The seated calf raise and tibialis raise stay. They're doing more for your right side than any core work will.
  • Better than cutting: do the planks in the rest gaps between calf raise sets. Low enough fatigue that it doesn't interfere, and it buys back five minutes.

Loading and progression

Log every set. Progression only works if you know what you lifted last week. Treat the first session of each lift as calibration — if the last rep of set one leaves three in the tank, the weight is right.

The four-week cycle

How the weight moves

  • Weeks 1–3: add 5kg per week to squat and RDL, 2.5kg to bench, row and overhead press, 2kg per hand to Bulgarians — as long as every rep is clean and the last set sits at RPE 8 or below.
  • Week 4: deload. Two sets instead of four, around 70% of working load, plyos at half volume.
  • Then repeat from your week 3 numbers.
  • Stall twice on the same weight? Drop 10% and build back. Normal, not failure.
Reading RPE

How hard each set should feel

  • RPE 7 — three reps left. Where the main lifts should live most weeks.
  • RPE 8 — two reps left. The top of a hard week.
  • RPE 9+ — one or none. Not needed here. Grinding reps costs recovery you'd rather spend on running.
  • Exception: curls, tricep extensions and planks can go close to failure. Small muscles, no recovery bill.
  • Accessories climb by reps first, load second.

Where these sit in the week

Placement logic matters more than the specific days. Move them to fit whatever the run week actually looks like.

Legs day

Placement, best to worst

  • Best: same day as a quality run, after it or six-plus hours later. Consolidating hard stress onto one day keeps easy days genuinely easy.
  • Workable: a standalone Wednesday, between a Tuesday quality session and a Thursday easy run. Costs two hard days back to back, but avoids doubles.
  • Avoid: anything within 24 hours before a long run or quality session. Saturday legs with a Sunday long run is the specific version never to do.
Upper day

Much more flexible

  • Goes anywhere, including the day before a hard run. Upper-body lifting barely touches running recovery.
  • Keep at least 48 hours between it and legs day so the trunk work isn't landing on a fatigued core.
  • If a week collapses and only one session happens, make it legs. Every time.

Cutting it down for a taper

Load maintains fitness, volume drains you. So cut sets, not weight.

Final 10 days

Halve the volume

  • Same exercises, same working weight, half the sets. Four sets becomes two.
  • Nothing above RPE 7. No stretching for a rep.
  • Drop the arm work and the third accessory pairing entirely.
Final week

Hard cutoffs

  • No plyometrics in the last 7 days. Not reduced — none.
  • Last legs session no closer than 5 days out.
  • Upper body can run to 3 days out, kept light.
  • Race week soreness is a planning failure, not a sign of a good block.

Why the plan looks like this

  • 01Low reps and real load, not circuits. The strength that improves running economy is neural — producing more force through the tissue you already have. That's a 4–6 rep job. High-rep burn work builds fatigue you then carry into your runs, which is the trade most runner strength plans get backwards.
  • 02The soleus is the priority accessory. Bent-knee calf raises load the soleus, which absorbs the single largest force in running and sits behind most calf and shin problems. Given the right side, it's the one exercise here never to skip.
  • 03Tibialis raises earn their spot. The shin muscle decelerates the foot on every landing. Undertrained in almost everyone and directly relevant to the shin history.
  • 04Plyometrics are here for stiffness, not power. The goal isn't jumping higher — it's a tendon that returns energy instead of absorbing it, which is free speed at the same effort. That adaptation is slow, which is why the ladder is measured in months.
  • 05Upper day leans on pulling, two-to-one plus face pulls, because running posture collapses forward under fatigue and nothing on a run trains the muscles that resist it.
  • 06The suitcase carry replaces the Pallof press. Load on one side forces the trunk to resist being pulled sideways for the whole walk — the same anti-rotation quality, in a more running-specific form, at no extra time cost.
  • 07Side plank matters more than front plank. It trains the lateral chain that resists pelvic drop when standing on one leg, which is what running is. It also doubles as an asymmetry check — if the right side gives out first, that's worth knowing.
  • 08Progress planks by difficulty, not duration. Past 45–60 seconds a plank becomes a boredom test. Long-lever position, shoulder taps, or a plate on the back. For side planks, lift the top leg into abduction.
  • 09Arms cost nothing that matters. Small muscles, negligible systemic fatigue, no interference with running. Take them close to failure if you want. Just keep them after the carry, since curls wreck the grip that the carry depends on.
  • 10Two sessions done for a year beats four done for a month. Lower-body strength dropping off is the single thing that correlates most directly with breakdown risk. Protect the frequency before chasing anything else.
